### The operand, and why it is not a second constant

```rust
let (design_rows, design_operand) = if model_name == "neuralprophet" {
    (span_days as usize, "span_days")
} else {
    (ds.len(), "points")
};
let design_cells = (design_rows + args.horizon) * holiday_columns;
```

Prophet materialises one design row per SUPPLIED `ds` entry, so a gap costs nothing. The NP
event design is built over the imputed daily grid `np::NpData::new` lays down across
`first..=last`, so its row count is `span_days` — always `>= len(ds)` and strictly greater on
every gappy series. Inheriting the prophet operand verbatim would under-price the gappy NP case
by exactly `span / points` (T-06.1-28), closing one under-pricing while opening another.

Both operands are bounded by 20 000 (`MAX_SPAN_DAYS` **is** `MAX_POINTS`), so the product is
`<= 2.365e7` under either arm and the existing no-overflow argument holds unchanged.

The prophet message is byte-identical to the shipped one — `"points"` substituted into the
operand slot reproduces `((points + horizon) x holiday_columns = (200 + 100) x 400)` exactly.
That is asserted, not assumed: `the_design_cost_refusal_names_the_operand_it_used_on_the_prophet_arm`
requires the prophet form, requires both factor VALUES, and requires the message NOT to claim it
priced on the span.

## FINDING 1 (routed here from 06.1-05, and it is worse than the routing says)

**Nothing in this plan's committed work depends on it, and it decides what Task 2 costs.**

06.1-05 measured that SC3's per-column recovery bar (`event_effect_recovery_rel`, 0.10) does not
hold at `n_lags > 0` under the epoch budget `np::door_epochs` configures, and holds at "320 (the
AR cap)". Two further facts, MEASURED here on the shipped rules:

**1. The door configures 320 for no series of 51 points or more.** `door_epochs` at `n_lags > 0`
is `min(auto_epochs(n_points), 320)`, and `auto_epochs` DECREASES with the point count:

| points | 10 | 50 | 51 | 200 | 365 | 1200 | 2400 | 5000 | 20000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| door budget at `n_lags > 0` | 320 | 320 | **320** | 190 | 160 | 110 | 90 | 70 | 50 |
| (`auto_epochs` before the cap) | 500 | 330 | 320 | 190 | 160 | 110 | 90 | 70 | 50 |

The cap binds only at `n <= 50`, against `MIN_POINTS = 10`. So the only budget at which the bar
was ever observed to hold is one the shipped door reaches for essentially no real series, and
the gap widens as the series lengthens — the opposite of the direction more data usually helps.

**2. Even at 320 the bar does not close.** In 06.1-05's own table, 2400 points at 320 epochs,
seed 7 reads **0.1663** against the 0.10 bar. One seed in four, at the budget the remedy would
move to.

**3. Raising the budget collides with C-08, a shipped bound.** Priced at `E = 6` event columns
through `np::request_train_cost`, against `MAX_NP_TRAIN_COST = 15 000 000`:

| points | `n_lags = 7` priced at the door budget | priced at 320 |
|---|---|---|
| 1200 | 2 362 140 (15.75 %) | 6 871 680 (45.81 %) |
| 2400 | 3 876 660 (25.84 %) | 13 783 680 (91.89 %) |
| 5000 | 6 291 180 (41.94 %) | **28 759 680 (191.73 %) — REFUSED** |
| 20000 | 17 993 700 (119.96 %) | 115 159 680 (767.73 %) |

A flat 320 would turn currently-accepted 5000-point requests into refusals. That is itself a
behaviour change on the published surface, so "just raise the budget" is not the cheap option it
reads as, and it does not even buy the bar.

This is recorded in `.planning/WINDOWS.md` as an open `unmet-truth` (id 25) and is surfaced at the
checkpoint below, because it changes what answering `proceed` commits to. It is **not** decided
here: the plan's own Task 2 step 8 directs that a bar failing under the door's configuration is a
FINDING to record and stop on, and the honest candidate resolutions (raise the budget; narrow what
the arm accepts; scope the bar to the configurations it holds at; refuse the combination) are not
equivalent and are not the executor's to pick alone.
